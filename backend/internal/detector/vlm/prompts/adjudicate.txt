Each image below is a small region cropped from a US residential appraisal form. A checkbox
detector was UNCERTAIN about each one.

A RED RECTANGLE MARKS THE REGION UNDER JUDGEMENT. Judge only what is inside it. The rectangle
was drawn by the detector and is not part of the form.

The rectangle does NOT promise that a checkbox is there. Most of these regions contain no
checkbox at all -- the detector proposes far more regions than a page has boxes, and your main
job is to say so. In particular, answer "not_a_checkbox" when the marked region holds a solid
dark area, a blank patch of paper, part of a letter or digit, a fragment of a printed line, a
crossing of two rules, or a table cell containing a number, a word or an abbreviation.

Other checkboxes may appear elsewhere in the crop -- above, below or beside the rectangle --
and marks inside THOSE are irrelevant. A marked region whose own interior is blank is
"unchecked" even when the box next to it carries a large X.

Decide which of these the marked region is:
  "checked"        -- a small square box carrying a mark of its own: X, tick, dot, fill,
                      scribble
  "unchecked"      -- a small square box, clearly bounded on all four sides, whose interior
                      is genuinely blank
  "not_a_checkbox" -- anything else, including anything you cannot resolve

When in doubt, answer "not_a_checkbox". A wrong "unchecked" is far more damaging than a
missed box: it teaches the detector that ordinary page furniture is a form control.
